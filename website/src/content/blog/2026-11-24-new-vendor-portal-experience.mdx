I am Philip, co-founder of Distr, which helps software and AI companies distribute their applications to self-hosted environments.
Our Open Source Software Distribution platform is available on GitHub ([`github.com/distr-sh/distr`](https://github.com/distr-sh/distr)).

[Distr 4.11](https://github.com/distr-sh/distr/releases/tag/4.11.0) is out, and it changes the [Vendor Portal](/docs/vendor-portal/) more than any release since we launched it. The old dashboard is gone, each customer has a page of its own and the settings that only concern your own team moved into tabs.

Almost all of this is frontend work. We needed hardly any backend changes, because the data was already there and we mostly changed where it shows up. The SDK and the controllers work exactly as before, and the only endpoint we removed is the one behind the old dashboard. On Distr Cloud you get the new Vendor Portal the next time you sign in. If you self-host, 4.11 upgrades like any other minor release.

## Why we reorganized the Vendor Portal around customers

For the last two years, every feature we shipped got its own page in the sidebar: licenses, secrets, notifications, support bundles, artifact downloads. That worked while there were few of them. With a dozen, each page showed one feature across all customers, and most of the time you wanted to know how one customer was doing.

To find out, you clicked through half a dozen pages and filtered each one by the same customer. We now start from the customer instead. You open one and find its deployments, licenses and everything else in one place.

## A home page that shows what needs attention

The new home page replaces the [dashboard we introduced in 2025](/blog/distr-dashboard/). The dashboard showed every deployment target as a card. The home page shows a few cards that count what needs a look:

- deployments that are failing or have stopped reporting, with the customers they belong to
- customers who still run or pull an outdated version of your application
- licenses that have expired, are about to expire or are missing
- support requests that are still open
- security advisories that are not resolved yet

A click on a card opens the page behind it: the customer list filtered by license or update status, the deployments that need attention, the open security advisories or your support requests. Further down are your customers with their status and an activity feed of recent events. New organizations also find the tutorials on this page.

## Everything about a customer on one page

A click on a customer in the new customer list opens its detail page. The overview at the top shows how the customer is doing. The tabs below it hold the customer's users, deployments, registry downloads, secrets, licenses, notifications and support bundles, plus a settings tab for the features you enable for them.

Licenses moved the furthest. The licenses page is gone, and entitlements and license keys now sit on the customer you issued them to, next to the deployments they cover.

The customer list has a search and filters for update status and license status. Those are the filters the updates and licenses cards on the home page open.

## Internal settings in one place

A few pages were only ever about your own organization: your team, the secrets of your internal deployments, domains and sign-in, subscription and billing. They are now tabs under Settings, which leaves the sidebar for the things you open every day.

## One deployments list instead of groups per customer

The deployments page used to group deployments by customer. Every customer has its own deployments tab now, so the deployments page shows one flat list across all of them. You can filter it by status, and a new "outdated" status finds the deployments that are behind the latest version.

## Opening a customer's portal with Customer Lens

When a customer reports a problem, it helps to see the screen they are looking at. [Customer Lens](/docs/platform/rbac/#customer-lens) opens the Customer Portal of any of your customers with your own role, and you don't need an invitation as one of their users. Partners can do the same for the customers assigned to them.

You find Customer Lens in the context switcher. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd> (<kbd>⌘</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd> on macOS) opens it from anywhere, and the same command palette also jumps to any page of the Vendor Portal. For the sidebar pages there are keyboard shortcuts as well.

## A new home page for the Customer Portal

Your customers get a home page too. After they sign in to the [Customer Portal](/docs/platform/customer-portal/), they see their deployments, or their artifacts if they only pull from your registry, together with the instructions you wrote for them. Depending on what you enabled for the customer, the page also shows their license and open support requests, and it reminds them to set up update notifications until they have. New customers also get a short checklist, for example to invite their team, create their first deployment or download an artifact.

The rest of the Customer Portal hasn't changed.

## Upgrading to Distr 4.11

You don't have to configure anything. Distr 4.11 adds no environment variables, and the SDK and the controllers are unchanged, so your integrations and your customers' deployments keep running as they are.

Some bookmarks will break, though. The old licenses page, for example, no longer exists, because its content now lives on the customer detail page.
